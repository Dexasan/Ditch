# DITCH — Pitch Deck
### Startcup Lazio 2026 — Slide-by-Slide Script

> **Format:** 12 slides, ~8–10 minutes with Q&A buffer.
> Each slide has: the visual direction, the headline, and exactly what to say.
> Use this as a speaker script — the slides themselves should be minimal (big visuals, few words).

---

## SLIDE 1 — TITLE

**Visual:** DITCH logo, clean dark background. No clutter.

**Headline:** *"Stream Once. Reach Everyone. Bring Them Back."*

**Say:**
> "This is DITCH. We're going to show you something that live streaming has needed for ten years and nobody has built yet."

---

## SLIDE 2 — THE MOMENT

**Visual:** Split screen. Left: a viewer watching on Twitch, anonymous in a chat moving too fast to read. Right: that same viewer, suddenly on screen, live, visible to thousands of people on five platforms at once.

**Headline:** *"What if watching wasn't enough?"*

**Say:**
> "There are 50 million live streamers in the world. And every single person watching them is stuck in a chat window — typing into a void. Nobody sees it. It doesn't matter.
>
> That's the interaction layer of live streaming in 2026. A viewer has no way to be present. They can only watch.
>
> DITCH changes that."

---

## SLIDE 3 — THE PROBLEM (2 halves)

**Visual:** Two boxes, side by side.
- Left: Platform logos with red X's — "Stream on Twitch OR YouTube OR TikTok"
- Right: Five different chat windows — "5 audiences, 5 conversations, no connection"

**Headline:** *"Two problems. Nobody has solved both."*

**Say:**
> "Creators face two structural problems.
>
> First: platform fragmentation. Twitch, YouTube, and TikTok all restrict you from streaming to competitors. You have to pick one. You're leaving audiences behind every single stream.
>
> Second: passive viewers. Even if you solve distribution, your audience is still scattered across five different chat windows. There's no mechanism for a viewer to matter. They watch. That's it.
>
> Tools like Restream fix the first problem — partially. Nobody has touched the second. And nobody has solved both together."

---

## SLIDE 4 — THE SOLUTION

**Visual:** One creator on screen. Around them: participant boxes — real people, from different platforms, all live. Bottom ticker shows unified chat. Platform logos across the top showing where the stream is going.

**Headline:** *"One stream. Every platform. Every viewer, present."*

**Say:**
> "DITCH is a browser-based live studio. The creator goes live once on DITCH.
>
> DITCH pushes that stream simultaneously to Twitch, YouTube, TikTok, and Kick — bypassing cross-streaming restrictions because DITCH is the origin, not a relay.
>
> Then: any viewer on any of those platforms can enter the creator's DITCH channel and appear live on screen. Not in chat. On screen. Their camera feed joins the broadcast and goes back out to every platform at once.
>
> The viewer is present. On thousands of screens. Simultaneously. That's the product."

---

## SLIDE 5 — HOW IT WORKS

**Visual:** Simple three-step diagram.
1. Creator goes live on DITCH (browser studio icon)
2. DITCH fans out to platforms (arrows to Twitch/YouTube/TikTok/Kick logos)
3. Viewer on TikTok enters DITCH → appears on all screens (camera icon feeding back into the broadcast)

**Headline:** *"Under 60 seconds from viewer to on-screen participant."*

**Say:**
> "Technically: DITCH builds the composite video stream in the browser using a proprietary canvas compositor — the hard problem we solved. It fans out through our relay infrastructure to every platform via RTMP.
>
> For the viewer: they click a link, allow camera access, and they're on stream in under 60 seconds. No app. No download. No hardware. Just a browser.
>
> That 60-second moment is the product. Everything else is infrastructure serving that moment."

---

## SLIDE 6 — WHY IT'S DEFENSIBLE

**Visual:** A Venn diagram. Circle A: "Multi-stream distribution" (Restream lives here). Circle B: "Viewer participation" (no one lives here). Center: DITCH.

**Headline:** *"We control the origin. Nobody else can."*

**Say:**
> "The reason nobody has built this is the same reason it's defensible.
>
> DITCH controls the composite stream origin. We build the frame — with participants baked in — before it reaches any platform. YouTube can't put a Twitch viewer on screen. Twitch can't put a TikTok viewer on screen.
>
> DITCH puts both on screen, simultaneously, on both platforms.
>
> You cannot replicate this from inside a single platform's ecosystem. The architecture is the moat."

---

## SLIDE 7 — GROWTH LOOP

**Visual:** Circular diagram. Creator streams → Viewer on Platform X sees participant → Viewer enters DITCH → Viewer becomes participant → Visible on all platforms → New viewers ask "how do I do that?" → back to start.

**Headline:** *"The audience acquires itself."*

**Say:**
> "Every viewer who appears on stream becomes a walking advertisement.
>
> They clip it. They post it. Their friends ask how to do it. Those friends go find the creator's stream, see the next participant, and the loop repeats.
>
> We don't need paid acquisition for viewers. The product is the growth mechanism. The creator's existing reach does the work."

## DESIGN NOTES

- **Color palette:** Dark background (#0a0a0a), white text, one accent color (suggest electric blue or a platform-neutral purple — not any single platform's color)
- **Fonts:** Clean sans-serif — Inter or Neue Haas Grotesk
- **Images:** Real product screenshots over mockups wherever possible — you have a live product, use it
- **Slide density:** Maximum 15 words per slide outside of speaker text. Let the visuals carry the story.
- **Slide 2 "the moment"** is the most important slide — if you have a screen recording of a viewer joining a live stream, play a 10-second clip here instead of a static image
